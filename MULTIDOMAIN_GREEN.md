# Multidomain Green operator: the extracellular branch plugs into the same machinery

**Status:** conceptual reduction of established cable/volume-conductor physics. Not a novelty claim.  
**Date:** 2026-08-15.

## 0. The useful synthesis

The extracellular side dig does not require a second theory beside `GREEN_NMDA_CLOSURE.md`.

It changes what we mean by the transfer operator `Z`.

In the ordinary Aizenbud/NEAT-style single-cell picture, extracellular voltage is effectively treated as a common reference and the interesting transfer is generated by the intracellular cable + membrane.

If extracellular voltage is solved self-consistently, membrane voltage is

```text
V_m = V_i - V_e.
```

Both `V_i` and `V_e` depend on transmembrane current.

Schematically in the frequency domain,

```text
V_i(omega) = Z_i(omega) I_m(omega)
V_e(omega) = Z_e(omega) I_m(omega)
```

so, subject to current-direction/sign conventions,

```text
V_m(omega) = [Z_i(omega) - Z_e(omega)] I_m(omega).
```

Call the resulting full transfer operator

```text
Z_full(omega).
```

The important structural difference is that `Z_i` inherits the dendritic cable/tree organization, whereas an extracellular volume-conductor term can couple membrane locations according to their **3D spatial arrangement**, not only their path along the dendritic tree.

Thus the intuitive sentence

> “different electrical material around the neuron changes what a local perturbation becomes”

survives as the much more ordinary statement

> **the local transfer impedance is a property of the coupled intracellular-membrane-extracellular system.**

---

## 1. Why a dense extracellular term can break a tree reduction

For a cable tree with a fixed extracellular reference, two membrane locations communicate through the cable operator defined on that tree.

For a volume conductor, extracellular potential at membrane location `i` receives contributions from transmembrane currents at many locations `j`:

```text
V_e,i ~= sum_j R_e(i,j) I_m,j
```

where `R_e(i,j)` depends on 3D geometry and the extracellular conductivity model.

In a simple homogeneous point/line-source approximation it decays with spatial separation rather than dendritic path distance.

Therefore `R_e` is generally dense.

A pair can be

```text
electrotonically far on the dendritic tree
but
spatially near in 3D.
```

That does not prove strong ephaptic coupling. It means the tree-only sparsity/factorization is no longer guaranteed once the extracellular field is allowed to feed back.

---

## 2. The frequency question becomes precise

Instead of saying “the fluid oscillates differently,” write

```text
Z_full(omega)
```

and ask which terms are frequency dependent.

In the conventional quasi-static resistive-volume-conductor approximation:

```text
Z_e(omega) ~= Z_e
```

while the membrane/cable/synaptic part has strong frequency dependence from capacitance and channel/synapse kinetics.

If a non-resistive extracellular/tissue model is justified by the measurement geometry, then `Z_e(omega)` can itself become frequency dependent.

Either way, a local impulse has the time-domain kernel

```text
h_ij(t) = F^{-1}[Z_full,ij(omega)].
```

So the original “one iota of time reveals a local spectrum” language reduces to an experimentally standard transfer-function statement.

---

## 3. NMDA closure survives unchanged in form

Once a physically appropriate `Z_full` is available, the local voltage-dependent current closure is still schematically

```text
v = Z_full [i_ext + I_N(v)].
```

The quasi-static susceptibility becomes

```text
Z_eff = (I - Z_full K)^(-1) Z_full
```

under the same inward-current sign convention used elsewhere in Dig.

This gives a clean place for ephaptic fields to matter:

```text
extracellular environment
    -> modifies Z_full
    -> changes local voltage produced by a synaptic event
    -> changes K(v) through NMDA Mg block
    -> changes nonlinear amplification
```

There is no need to invent an independent “field computation” vocabulary.

---

## 4. A small extracellular voltage can matter most near a nonlinear boundary

A useful subtlety follows.

Suppose ephaptic feedback changes a local dendritic voltage by only a fraction of a millivolt. Far from any nonlinear threshold that may be negligible.

Near a steep NMDA susceptibility region, however,

```text
dK/dv
```

can be large.

Then the same small `delta V_e` can produce a larger change in the local feedback gain than its raw voltage amplitude suggests.

This does **not** establish that physiological ephaptic fields alter Aizenbud FCI. It identifies the state in which a magnitude screen should be performed:

```text
not only at rest,
but near the local NMDA knee.
```

That is a better test than asking whether the extracellular field is “large” in isolation.

## 5. A hierarchy of extracellular tests

Do not jump to a full EMI simulation.

### E0 — geometry opportunity

Find membrane-location pairs that are tree/electrically far but 3D-near.

### E1 — forward field

Using the original intracellular simulation, compute `V_e(t)` at those membrane locations with a standard volume-conductor / line-source model.

### E2 — imposed-field replay

Feed the measured/computed `V_e(t)` back as a prescribed extracellular boundary field while keeping the intracellular current model otherwise fixed.

Measure change in local NMDA recruitment and somatic response.

### E3 — self-consistent coupling

Only if E2 is large enough to matter, solve the coupled intracellular-membrane-extracellular problem self-consistently (for example with an EMI-type formulation).

This ordering distinguishes

```text
morphology shapes the field
from
field feeds back enough to alter computation.
```

---

## 6. A useful null

If E1 shows that the self-generated extracellular voltage at tree-far / 3D-near partner branches is orders of magnitude below what is needed to move the NMDA response curve, retire the single-cell self-ephaptic branch.

Population ephaptic effects can remain biologically real without being relevant to the isolated-neuron FCI mechanism.

That is the likely null to beat.

## Current sentence

> **Extracellular space does not give Dig a second mechanism; it gives the Green operator another domain. Replace the tree-only `Z` by a coupled `Z_full` containing a dense 3D extracellular contribution, measure whether that correction is big enough to move local NMDA susceptibility, and stop if it is not.**
